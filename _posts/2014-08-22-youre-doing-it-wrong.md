---
title: "You’re doing it wrong.."
summary: "I spent two days building the wrong thing because my understanding of the requirements didn't match the client's — and thought about how to stop that happening again."
date: 2014-08-22
---

Not sure how I managed this, but I have just spent the last two days building the wrong thing.

Sure it wasn’t fair off, but as the a contractor this is not something that I want to be doing regularly. So how did it happen?

As I said to my boss it wasn’t intentional, it was a case of this is what I thought was required and I started building it and then dived into the code to make it work the way I thought they wanted.

Then, during a review it turned out that the client requirements differed to my expectations of them. Luckily enough in this instance the work that I did will help to meet a future requirements, so it wasn’t a complete waste.

So for me the question that I need to ask is, how did I let this happen? and more importantly, how can I avoid doing the same thing in the future?

Unfortunately I don’t think there are any hard and fast solutions, I think that there are things I can do to reduce the risk of this happening again (like regular discussions with the client about the requirements and implementation). I also think the only solution is constant effort, far more than was required when I was a permanent staff member, but that fine, after all, as they say, “that is why I get paid the big bucks”. :)
